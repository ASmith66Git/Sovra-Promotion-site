## Why We Don't Build for Traditional Desktops
We chose not to build for traditional desktops. That was a deliberate decision.
We don't have a Windows app. We don't have an app for legacy, non-integrated PCs. We have been asked about this many times, and the answer is not that we haven't got around to it—it's that we don't believe the fragmented desktop ecosystem is where the future of personal computing lives.
Instead, our focus is entirely dictated by **vertical integration**. We build exclusively for environments where the software and the hardware—specifically the Neural Processing Unit (NPU)—are co-designed from the ground up.
Because of this, we have made a singular exception for the **Apple Silicon ecosystem**. Whether it is an iPhone, an iPad, or a Mac running Apple Silicon, the unified memory architecture and tightly coupled NPU allow us to run incredibly complex AI models locally, with uncompromising performance. On these devices, the hardware and software speak the same language.
Traditional desktop platforms, by contrast, are fundamentally fragmented. Building for them means building for a context running AI in the cloud because you cannot assume a standardized, high-performance local NPU. That undermines everything we care about regarding privacy, speed, and context.
The personal, integrated device—the Apple Silicon laptop you open instantly, the phone you carry everywhere, the tablet you reach for first—is the primary computer for the future we are building. The sensors are richer, the power efficiency is unmatched, and the always-on, always-connected nature of these integrated devices is exactly what a digital second brain needs to be.
So we made a choice: go deep where the hardware matches our software ambitions, and leave the fragmented desktop world behind. We think in five years, this will seem obvious.
